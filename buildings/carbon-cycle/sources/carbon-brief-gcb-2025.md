## What it says

All figures are 2025 projections from the November 2025 release, in GtCO2.

- Total CO2 (fossil + land use): 42.2 GtCO2, about −0.04% on 2024, effectively tied with 2024 as a record.
- Fossil CO2 incl. cement: 38.1 GtCO2, +1.1% (range 0.2% to 2.2%). Coal +0.8%, oil +1.0%, gas +1.3%. Shares: coal 42%, oil 33%, gas 21%; cement about 3.8% of global emissions before the carbonation sink, 1.9% after.
- Land-use change: 4.1 GtCO2, down 9.8% (about 0.5 GtCO2 lower than 2024), with large uncertainties. 2015–2024 average about 5.0 GtCO2.
- By country, 2025 change (range): China (32% share) +0.4% (−0.9 to +2.0); USA (13%) +1.9% (−0.2 to +4.1), about 40% of the global increase, coal +7.5%; India (8%) +1.4% (−0.3 to +3.1); EU (6%) +0.4% (−2.1 to +2.8); rest of world (38%) +1.1% (−1.1 to +3.3). International aviation +6.8%, shipping flat.
- 2025 CO2 growth about 2.3 ppm, below the 2015–2024 average of 2.6 ppm and well below 2024's record 3.7 ppm. 2025 concentration 425.7 ppm.
- 2015–2024: ocean took about 29% of emissions (~11.8 GtCO2/yr), land about 21% (~8.7 GtCO2/yr), atmosphere kept about 49% (~20.4 GtCO2/yr).
- Land sink "appears to have recovered to its pre-El Niño strength" in 2025, helped by low global fire emissions.
- On the 2024 imbalance: the sum of sinks was notably larger than estimated emissions; possible causes listed are incomplete 2024 land-cover data, missing fire emissions, or a possibly high NOAA growth rate.
- Companion Nature study: climate change made the sinks about 15% weaker over 2015–2024 than they would otherwise be (land 25%, ocean 7%).
- Remaining budgets from 2025 (2024 warming 1.36 °C): 1.5 °C 170 GtCO2 (~4 years); 1.7 °C 525 GtCO2 (~12 years); 2 °C 1,055 GtCO2 (~25 years).
- 35 countries had declining emissions over 2015–2024, together 27% of global emissions, versus 18 countries in 2005–2014.

## Agent analysis (Ravi, sources desk)

- Useful for the country and fuel breakdown of the 2025 projection, which the paper abstract does not give. Treat as secondary; the per-country ranges are the GCB's own projection ranges relayed by Carbon Brief.
- Note the 2025 numbers were later revised in the final article (+1.0%, not +1.1%). The country-level 2025 figures here have not been checked against the final paper.
- The 2025 total of 42.2 GtCO2 (11.5 GtC) being flat on 2024 only because land-use emissions fell is the kind of statement that depends entirely on the ±0.7 GtC land-use uncertainty. A 0.5 GtCO2 (0.14 GtC) change in E_LUC is well inside its error bar.
