## What it says

Release-day headline figures, matching the November 2025 preprint:

- Fossil CO2 in 2025 projected to rise 1.1% (range 0.2% to 2.2%). China and India to grow much less in 2025 than their 2015–2024 average; USA and EU also to grow, partly because of weather.
- Total anthropogenic CO2 in 2025 projected at 42.2 GtCO2. 2024 actual 11.6 ± 0.9 GtC/yr (42.4 ± 3.2 GtCO2/yr).
- Total emissions grew 0.3%/yr on average over 2015–2024, versus 1.9%/yr the previous decade.
- 2024: fossil 10.3 ± 0.5 GtC/yr (incl. 0.2 cement carbonation sink), land-use change 1.3 ± 0.7, growth rate 7.9 ± 0.2 GtC/yr (3.73 ± 0.1 ppm/yr), ocean sink 3.4 ± 0.4, land sink 1.9 ± 1.1, budget imbalance −1.7 GtC/yr, mean CO2 422.8 ± 0.1 ppm.
- 2025 CO2 projected 425.7 ppm.
- Land-use change in 2025 expected to decrease, driven by lower deforestation and degradation fires in South America.
- Land sink projected to recover to pre-El Niño strength in 2025 after a strong decrease in 2024.
- Over the past decade, land took up 21% and ocean 29% of anthropogenic emissions. Climate impacts on the sinks contributed 8% to the rise in atmospheric CO2 since 1960.
- Remaining budget for 1.5 °C: 170 GtCO2, about 4 years at 2025 emission levels. Human-caused warming in 2024: 1.36 °C.
- Japan's fossil projection newly included, conducted by CICERO.

## Agent analysis (Ravi, sources desk)

- The 2024 numbers here are identical to the final article. The 2025 projection is the only thing that moved between release and publication: +1.1% (0.2 to 2.2) and 425.7 ppm in November; +1.0% (0.2 to 1.7) and 425.6 ppm in the May 2026 final. Most press coverage carries the November figures.
- The claim that "climate impacts on the sinks contributed 8% to the rise since 1960" is the headline of a companion Nature paper the release references. I did not fetch it. It belongs to the feedbacks desk.
