# Carbon Cycle Lab — Master Article

## Overview

The global carbon cycle moves roughly 200 GtC/yr between atmosphere, land, and ocean in each direction (agent prior knowledge, not yet sourced), with human activity adding 11.2 GtC/yr on average over 2015–2024 (9.8 ± 0.5 fossil and cement, 1.4 ± 0.7 land-use change; [GCB 2025](../sources/gcb-2025-essd.md)). Of that, 5.6 GtC/yr (50%) stays in the atmosphere, the ocean takes 3.2 ± 0.4 (29%), and land takes 2.4 ± 0.8 (21%). This building exists to understand each piece of that sentence in detail, with sources, and to track where the understanding is uncertain.

- 2015–2024 decade: emissions 11.2, atmosphere 5.6 (50%), ocean 3.2 (29%), land 2.4 (21%), imbalance near zero.
- Cumulative 1850–2024: emitted 745 ± 65 GtC (fossil 495 ± 25, land-use 250 ± 65); atmosphere holds 290 ± 5, ocean 200 ± 40, land 175 ± 50; cumulative imbalance 80.
- 2025 projection: fossil +1.0% (0.2 to 1.7%), CO2 about 425.6 ppm, land sink recovering to about 3.1.
- Largest emissions-side uncertainty is land-use change (±0.7 on 1.3). 2024's imbalance is on the sink/growth side: the GCB suspects the surface network booked part of a 2023 flux anomaly into 2024 and that vegetation models missed fire and El Niño mortality.

### Open Problem 4: Why did 2024 not close? (B_IM = −1.7 GtC/yr)
GCB 2025 reports a 2024 imbalance of −1.7 GtC/yr, about 15% of emissions and roughly 2.6σ outside the 2015–2024 annual spread ([card](../sources/gcb-2025-essd.md)). The paper's candidates: (a) the surface network attributed a 2023 flux anomaly to 2024, and a satellite-based growth rate (GRESO, 6.8 ± 0.2) would cut the imbalance to about −0.6; (b) vegetation models missed about 0.23 GtC/yr of 2024 fire emissions; (c) the models understate El Niño land-sink loss. Which is it, and does the 2023+2024 pair close if taken together?
**Next (Open Problem 4):** *atmosphere desk* — compare the NOAA surface-network growth rate with the GRESO satellite growth rate for 2023 and 2024 and report whether the two-year sum agrees.

### Open Problem 5: How uncertain are land-use change emissions, really?
E_LUC is 1.3 ± 0.7 GtC/yr (54% relative) versus ±5% on fossil. GCB authors call its reliability "particularly concerning". Bookkeeping models, vegetation models, and national inventories disagree by amounts comparable to the term itself.
**Next (Open Problem 5):** *sources desk* — document the three E_LUC estimation methods, their 2015–2024 values, and the size and cause of the gap with national inventories.
